Google Reviews for Clinics in Dubai: The DHA-Safe Guide

By Abu Bakar / October 12, 2026

When someone searches for a skin clinic or aesthetic clinic in Dubai, the shortlist forms quickly. A patient looks at the map results, glances at the star rating, and reads two or three recent Google reviews before deciding who to message. For a treatment people take seriously, that trust check matters even more than it does for a salon visit.

Clinics face two problems that salons do not. First, patients are often private about their treatments, so many simply never think to post. Second, healthcare is a regulated space: the way you reply to a review, or reuse one in your marketing, needs more care. This guide covers Google reviews for clinics in Dubai: how to ask without breaking Google’s rules, how to reply without exposing anyone’s privacy, and where regulated advertising begins.

🔑 Key takeaways

  • You can invite every patient to review you with a link or QR code. You cannot reward them for doing it.
  • Replies are public. Keep them short, general and free of any detail that confirms someone was a patient or what they had done.
  • Reviews on your Google profile and reviews reused in your own advertising are two different things. Check Dubai Health Authority (DHA) guidance before the second.
  • A steady, administrative process (a post-visit message with the link, a weekly reply slot) beats any one-off push.

Why Clinic Reviews Are Different From Salon Reviews

A salon client is happy to tag the stylist and post a photo of the finished colour. A patient who has had a consultation or a treatment is much less likely to announce it. That means clinics usually get fewer reviews per visit, and the ones that do arrive carry extra weight with the next patient.

The same privacy that makes reviews scarce also shapes what you can say back. Your reply is visible to anyone, so it should never confirm that a particular person was your patient. If you want the salon-side version of this process, we wrote it up in our guide for salons; the clinic version changes the replies and the marketing use, not the ethics of asking.

Reviews also feed local visibility. They are one input, alongside relevance and distance, that we cover in why your clinic may not show in Google Maps searches.

Google Reviews for Clinics: What Google Allows and Prohibits

Google’s Business Profile help says that you can ask customers to visit a Google link or scan a QR code to leave a review, and that reviews must reflect a genuine experience. The same page states that offering incentives, such as free or discounted goods or services, in exchange for posting, changing or removing reviews is fake and misleading content and is strictly prohibited. Google also describes how to create a link or QR code to request reviews, and notes that it can be shared by email or WhatsApp.

  • Do: use your official review link or QR code and send it to every patient.
  • Don’t: offer a discounted session, a free add-on, a gift or loyalty credit in return for a review.
  • Don’t: send the link only to patients you expect to be pleased. Google expects reviews to reflect real experience, and selective asking skews that.
  • Don’t: have staff, relatives or friends post about visits that never happened.
  • Don’t: buy reviews or use any service promising a set number.

Asking Patients Without Pressure

Keep the request administrative: a thank-you for visiting and a link. It should come from reception or your messaging system, not mid-consultation, and it should say nothing about the outcome of a treatment.

  • Same-day message: “Thank you for visiting [Clinic] today. If you would like to share your experience of the service, here is our Google review link: [link]. Feedback is always welcome.”
  • QR card at reception: a small card on the desk for patients who prefer to do it on the spot.
  • One reminder at most: a polite note a few days later, then stop.

Avoid wording that steers the content, such as asking for “five stars” or suggesting what to write about the results. Asking for the experience of booking, waiting time and staff courtesy keeps the invitation neutral and keeps you clear of anything resembling an outcome claim.

Replying Without Exposing Patient Details

Google’s guidance on replying says to keep replies short, personalise them, respond in good time, and not to share a reviewer’s private information. For a clinic, take that one step further: write each reply as if a stranger were reading it, because they are.

Reply language: safer vs. riskier for clinics

Situation Safer reply Riskier reply
Positive review “Thank you for your kind words and for visiting us.” “Glad your filler turned out so well!”
Complaint about waiting time “We’re sorry about the wait. Please contact our team so we can look into it.” “Your appointment was delayed because of your earlier procedure.”
Complaint about a treatment “We take feedback seriously. Please contact us directly to discuss.” Explaining what was done and why, in public
Reviewer you can’t identify “We can’t find a visit under this name. Please contact us.” Confirming or denying that they were a patient

Reviews that raise a clinical concern deserve a calm, private follow-up, not a public debate. We cover the full approach in what to say when a patient leaves a bad review. Also avoid using replies to advertise: Google advises against using a response to promote deals.

Using Reviews in Your Own Marketing

A review sitting on your Google profile is one thing. Copying it into an Instagram post, an ad or a landing page is your own advertising, and healthcare advertising in Dubai is regulated by the Dubai Health Authority (DHA). We have not found a simple rule we can quote here, so treat this as a decision for your licensed clinic manager: check the current DHA advertising guidance, or ask the authority, before putting patient reviews into promotional material. Never edit a review, pair it with a treatment result image, or imply a guaranteed outcome.

Practical safe default: let reviews do their work on Google, link to your profile from your website, and keep promotional content focused on the service experience, team and facilities. Our complete guide to clinic marketing in the UAE explains the wider compliance picture.

A 15-Minute Weekly Routine

Put one named person in charge and give them a fixed slot, for example Sunday morning:

  • Check for new reviews and reply to each in the general, confidential style above.
  • Flag anything that needs a private clinical or service follow-up and pass it to the manager.
  • Check that the review link and QR card still work and that the post-visit message is going out.
  • Note the review count, so you can see the trend month on month. There is no magic number, only a steady upward line.

Frequently asked questions

Can an aesthetic clinic in Dubai ask patients for Google reviews?

Yes. Google’s guidance says businesses can ask customers to leave a review by sharing a Google link or QR code. What is not allowed is offering anything in exchange for a review. Clinics should also check Dubai Health Authority (DHA) advertising guidance before reusing patient reviews in their own marketing.

Can we offer a discount on the next treatment for a Google review?

No. Google classes free or discounted goods or services offered in exchange for reviews as fake and misleading content and strictly prohibits them. That covers reviews posted, changed or removed, so a reward for any of these puts the profile at risk.

How should a clinic reply to a Google review without breaching patient confidentiality?

Keep the reply general. Thank the reviewer, avoid naming the treatment, the practitioner’s clinical decisions or anything that confirms they were a patient, and invite them to contact the clinic directly. Google also advises against sharing a reviewer’s private information in a reply.

Is it okay to only ask patients who seem happy to leave a review?

No. Google expects reviews to reflect genuine experiences, and sending only satisfied patients to your profile distorts that picture. Give every patient the same simple invitation and handle complaints through a private channel.

Can a clinic repost patient Google reviews on Instagram or its website?

Be cautious. A review on Google is the patient’s own content, but featuring it in your own advertising is a different matter and healthcare advertising is regulated. Check the current Dubai Health Authority (DHA) advertising rules, or ask the authority directly, before using patient reviews in promotional material.
